Gold and silver prospecting

Native gold and silver are nuggets buried inside thin quartz slabs. This page works out where they are, what the prospecting pick can and cannot tell you about them, and how to dig for them. Yields are ore blocks per 100 durability, counting every block dug, every node search and every nugget broken.

The guide

  1. Bring a tier-3 pickaxe (tin, bismuth or black bronze, or better). Quartz, native gold and native silver all need mining tier 3. A copper pickaxe cannot open the slab.
  2. Pick where to dig, best first: a loose gold or silver nugget on the ground; a loose quartz stone where a 3-durability reading says "Miniscule amounts of" gold or silver; a loose quartz stone; a reading with that mention; anywhere.
  3. Dig a shaft and node-search every 13 blocks. At a loose stone the slab is shallow: search at 6 and 19 blocks down. Elsewhere go to about 120. Any gold or silver in the result, even "trace", means a slab is within 6 blocks.
  4. On a hit, find the slab level with the edge search (binary search up the shaft, as for copper), tunnel to the quartz, and dig four lanes beside your tunnel at 3 and 6 blocks either side across the 13-block patch. Search again after each lane and stop when nothing is left.
  5. Then run four arms along the slab from the entry point, 40 to 64 blocks each, at slab level (it tilts less than one block per block), searching every 13. Repeat step 4 at every hit.
  6. Skip slabs the shaft found empty. Blind sweeps cost as much as they return. Dig the next shaft about 48 blocks away, and expect the fourth shaft in a row to return about 70% of the first.

Copper with the same accounting gives about 26 ore blocks per 100 durability. Gold and silver are several times worse everywhere, and the best cases come from what you can see on the ground, not from the pick.

What the game builds

Quartz slabs

Quartz is a flat disc, radius about 32, placed at any depth. In these worlds slabs hold 98% of all quartz. A slab is usually one block thick ( of columns are 1 to 3 blocks), tilts by at most one block between neighbours in 99% of places, and covers about columns. of the map has a slab somewhere below it.

Gold and silver are child deposits. The generator gives each child the parent's placed quartz blocks as its host, so every nugget replaces a quartz block inside the slab ( touch another quartz block). A cluster tries 20 random spots in a small cube and keeps the ones that land in the slab, about 4.6 nuggets on average, 15 at most.

How deep the slabs are

Blocks below the surface to the top of the slab, all worlds. Search every 13 blocks covers every depth; the shaft is the main cost.

What the pick tells you

Node search: presence only

The game words counts as trace (under 10), small, medium and up. A cluster has at most about 15 nuggets, so nearly every search that finds anything says "trace". The copper routine steers tunnels by rising counts, and that is not available here. Everything below uses yes or no. In the richest ground of positive searches would read above trace, so this is conservative there.

Density reading: almost blind, but not useless

Gold and silver readings are the ore map times the share of the rock column that is quartz, which is tiny. Readings above 0.025 (a density word) hardly ever occur. Between 0.002 and 0.025 the game only prints "Miniscule amounts of gold" or silver. That mention is the useful part.

Loose stones: free information

Using the game's placement rules, expect about loose quartz stones per km², one per three chunks. They sit over slabs within about 9 blocks of the surface, which is of the map, and of slabs get at least one. What the numbers say

Arms after a hit are the whole game. Harvesting only around the first hit yields under half. Four lanes beat two by about 20%, and no lanes lose half. Search every 13 is right; every 16 or 19 loses 10 to 20%. Arms of 64 are no better than 40; arms of 24 are worse. Depth makes little difference per durability, so keep going while the shaft is cheap.

Sweeping every slab the shaft touches finds something on more shafts but returns the same per durability, and worse from a loose stone. Perfect aim at each hit would be worth 1.5 to 1.7 times more, so a better way to locate a cluster from yes or no answers is the largest open gain.

What this does not cover

Durability is pooled, as in the copper report. Node searches are only 6 to 10% of it; nearly all is digging, so your pickaxe wears out long before the prospecting pick. A tin bronze prospecting pick has 250 durability, iron 650, steel 1625; those numbers are the budget only for searches.

Simplifications: caves are ignored (shafts would be cheaper); the tunnel follows the slab exactly; harvest lanes are a fixed pattern, not adaptive aiming; a tunnel step costs 2 for its two-block height. Silver inside galena essentially never generated (0 to 54 blocks per world) and is left out.

Untested: other node-search radii (radius 8 would allow probes about 17 apart), other deposit spawn rates, and using small/medium words in rich ground to steer. Results come from three worlds; per-world values are in the data file.
